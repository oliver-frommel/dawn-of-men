# Research Notes

## Australopithecus deyiremeda

A paper published in *Nature* reports that a 3.4-million-year-old partial foot,
found in Ethiopia in 2009, belongs to the ancient human relative
*Australopithecus deyiremeda*. The paper describes this species as a more
primitive member of *Australopithecus* than the famous "Lucy" species,
*Australopithecus afarensis*.

The identification resolves a long-running mystery surrounding the fossil and
provides new insight into the lifestyles and diversity of ancient hominins
living in eastern Africa approximately 3 to 4 million years ago.

### Potential Game Relevance

- Multiple australopith species may coexist in eastern Africa during the game's
  early period.
- *A. deyiremeda* can be represented as a distinct neighboring lineage rather
  than a stage in the direct playable lineage.
- Differences in foot anatomy may support distinct movement, climbing, or
  habitat-use mechanics.

## Early fire use and control

- Possible fire use around 2 Ma is a hypothesis. At Koobi Fora, Kenya, researchers examined evidence of fire associated with a roughly 1.5 Ma *Homo erectus* site. [Koobi Fora study](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/692530)
- At Wonderwerk Cave, South Africa, burned bone and plant ash establish burning inside the cave around 1 Ma. The evidence establishes burning in an archaeological context. Attribution to a particular species and the ability to ignite fire remain open. [Wonderwerk Cave study](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1117620109)
- Burned seeds, wood, and flint at Gesher Benot Ya’aqov in present-day Israel provide evidence consistent with controlled use around 790 ka. [Gesher Benot Ya’aqov study](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.304.5671.663a)

The game uses 2 Ma for a chronicle entry on possible early use and 1 Ma as the threshold for depicting a tended hearth.

## Opportunities after wildfire

Observations of savanna chimpanzees link burned ground with better foraging opportunities. This provides a living example of how a primate can benefit from fire by visiting the area after it passes. It supports a hypothesis about early hominin behavior. [Savanna chimpanzee field study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0047248422000537)

Fresh growth can draw grazers after a burn. Fieldwork in Tanzania’s Serengeti found that use of burn patches varied among grazer species and changed as the growing season progressed. [Serengeti grazer study](https://doi.org/10.1002/bes2.2216)

For the game, distinguish a cooled burn with newly exposed food from later regrowth with fresh shoots and more grazer activity. Both opportunities can occur before the player learns to keep fire. Recovering an ember for a hearth belongs to the later fire progression. The game can use seasonal transitions to represent these short ecological changes within the longer chronicle.

## Sahelanthropus tchadensis and a Late Miocene opening

*Sahelanthropus tchadensis* is known from Toros-Ménalla in present-day Chad, in Central Africa. One dating study placed the fossil-bearing unit at approximately 6.8–7.2 million years ago. The site preserves evidence of a varied environment with lakeside habitats. [Discovery paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature00879) · [Dating study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18305174/) · [Site geology and fauna](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature00880)

Studies of the femur and forearm bones support some bipedal locomotion alongside climbing. The precise mix of movement remains debated, and the fossil evidence leaves its relationship to later hominins open. [2022 limb study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-04901-z) · [2026 reanalysis](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adv0130)

### Potential Game Relevance

- Offer an optional Late Miocene chapter in the Chad Basin around 7 Ma, with one playable hominin. Use lakeshore and woodland routes for foraging and exploration. Dry land remains the route around water.
- Make the chapter about how early hominins used the landscape. Keep hearth skills unavailable. Walking and climbing are plausible capabilities, with their exact reconstruction treated as a design interpretation.
- Link Chad to the later Rift Valley opening through the chronicle as a change of population, place, and time. The transition leaves the relationship between the populations open.
- Use chapter dates for species and technology gates and local wet/dry seasons for play. At the current 100,000-year step, moving the start from 3.2 Ma to 7 Ma would add 38 turns. A short prologue can cover a meaningful period of local play before the chronicle jumps forward.

## Orrorin tugenensis

The Smithsonian places *Orrorin tugenensis* at about 6.2–5.8 million years ago. Its fossils come from the Tugen Hills in central Kenya. The shape of the upper femur supports walking on two legs, and upper-limb fossils are consistent with climbing. Its relationship to later hominins remains unresolved. [Smithsonian species profile](https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species/orrorin-tugenensis) · [Comparative femur study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18356526/)

### Potential Game Relevance

Give *Orrorin* a Late Miocene chapter in the Tugen Hills around 6 Ma, between the proposed Chad chapter around 7 Ma and the existing Rift Valley opening at 3.2 Ma. Ground travel between foraging areas and optional climbs to reach food or shelter would let the player use both forms of movement. Use the chronicle to shift between the populations and leave their evolutionary relationships open.
